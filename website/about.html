About re.inform

Our story, methodology, and frequently asked questions

Project Background

re.inform emerged from the recognition that traditional approaches to countering extremism often fail because they don't account for differences in how people process information and form beliefs. Our project combines insights from psychology, sociology, and communication studies to develop more effective engagement strategies.

Our Team

re.inform is a collaborative project bringing together researchers, communicators, and technologists passionate about reducing the influence of harmful ideologies. Our team members include specialists in:

  • Social psychology and belief formation
  • Online community dynamics
  • Communication strategies
  • Digital tool development

Methodology Details

Personality Classification

We've identified several key personality factors that influence susceptibility to extremist messaging, including:

  • Need for cognitive closure
  • Tolerance for ambiguity
  • Trust orientation
  • Value hierarchies
  • Identity attachment patterns

Digital "Macroscope"

Our digital analysis maps connections between concepts and communities online, allowing us to:

  • Identify common pathways to radicalization
  • Understand linguistic patterns that resonate with different groups
  • Track the evolution of extremist narratives
  • Find potential intervention points

Counter-Narrative Development

We use an evidence-based process to create engagement strategies:

  • Identify underlying needs and worldviews
  • Develop framing that addresses those needs
  • Test with focus groups and digital analysis
  • Refine based on field feedback

Frequently Asked Questions

Isn't categorizing people problematic?

We recognize the ethical concerns around categorization and work to avoid reductive approaches. Our framework is:

  • Descriptive, not prescriptive
  • Based on tendencies, not fixed traits
  • Focused on communication needs, not value judgments
  • Continuously refined through research and feedback

How is this different from manipulation?

There's an important ethical distinction between tailoring communication to be more accessible and manipulating people. Our approach:

  • Aims to foster critical thinking, not push specific conclusions
  • Is transparent about its methods and goals
  • Respects individual agency
  • Is grounded in evidence-based psychology

Does this approach work for all types of extremism?

While our current focus is primarily on far-right extremism, fascism, racism, and climate denialism, the underlying psychological principles can be applied more broadly. We're continuously expanding our research to encompass different forms of harmful ideologies.
